Why a bridal shower checklist starts with the wedding, not the shower

A bridal shower borrows its two biggest constraints from the wedding: the date has to sit clear of the wedding itself and of other pre-wedding events, and the guest list is normally drawn from people already invited to the wedding. Settling both against the wedding plan first is what stops a shower from being rescheduled or its invitations from causing awkwardness later.

The bridal shower planning checklist, in order

  1. Agree who is hosting and what the budget is. Showers are often co-hosted by the maid of honour, the bridal party, or family. Settle who pays for what before anything is booked, because every later step spends from that budget.
  2. Fix the date against the wedding calendar. Ask the bride which weekends are clear of the wedding, the bachelorette, and travel. Showers commonly land in the weeks before the wedding; the exact window is the bride's call, not a rule.
  3. Build the guest list from the wedding guest list. Get the list from the bride or the couple. Inviting someone to the shower who is not invited to the wedding is the etiquette problem most worth avoiding, and the list is also what decides the venue size.
  4. Choose and confirm the venue. A home, a hotel or venue room, or a private space at a restaurant. Confirm it in writing, including the time you can get in to set up, before moving on.
  5. Send invitations. Give guests several weeks of notice, and more if guests are travelling into Memphis or the date sits near a holiday weekend.
  6. Choose the palette or theme with the bride. Do it after the venue is confirmed. Many brides want the shower to echo the wedding palette without repeating it; a palette chosen against a room you have seen holds up.
  7. Plan the food and drink format. Brunch, light bites, or a grazing table. Format decides quantity, service, and how much table space the room gives up, so it comes before decor.
  8. Book decor and setup. With the venue, count and palette settled, a decor quote is fast and accurate. Porch & Party celebration setups start at $300+ and grazing table styling starts at $250+, food not included by default.
  9. Plan games, gifts and the order of the afternoon. Gift opening takes longer than hosts expect with a large guest list; decide whether it happens at the shower and build the timeline around it.
  10. Confirm everything one week out. Re-confirm the venue, the final count, the food order, and the setup access window.
  11. Set the day-of timeline. Work backwards from guest arrival: venue access, decor install, food placement, then guests. Give the install more room than you think it needs.

If you have less time than this assumes, keep the order and compress the gaps. The order is what prevents rework.

What this means in practice

The two steps hosts most often get out of order are the guest list and the decor. A guest list built before the wedding list is final causes the awkward invitation problem, and decor booked before the venue is confirmed has to be redone when the room turns out to be a different size or available later than planned. Settle the wedding-borrowed decisions first and the rest of the list goes in one pass.
